Iron ore and steel

The pen on the glass

Situation
An iron ore terminal designed for single-trailer trucks started receiving B-doubles and road trains. The queue spilled onto the public road, the client was pressing, and everyone had the same diagnosis: not enough staff.
What I did
I spent an hour standing at a single point of the operation, just watching. Each operator owned a truck, not a step. I redrew the roles by step on the glass wall of the meeting room, with the shift leader, and the drawing became the standard.
Result
Close to 50% more unloading capacity, with the same team and no investment.
Grains

Every wagon at its own 100%

Situation
A mixed fleet of wagon types, loading without a standard and, at the peak of the harvest, demand above capacity. Every tonne not loaded was lost.
What I did
Each wagon got its own weight target, set as a percentage of its own payload limit. The production meeting moved out of the office into a room inside the operation, at shift change.
Result
Over 100 tonnes more per train with the same assets, and millions of reais in additional revenue in the first year.
Iron ore and steel

Ten ores in three yards

Situation
A terminal acted as the forward stockyard for a steel mill's blend, handling up to ten different ore products to chemical specification.
What I did
Segregation control by product and by yard, with traceability and unloading windows.
Result
No cross-contamination, at a terminal on which the client's daily revenue depended.
Grains

Turning a terminal around

Situation
A grain terminal with the worst sanitary audit record in the unit.
What I did
An integrated management routine, a standard for each step and close follow-up as acting manager.
Result
The highest score in sanitary audits, the terminal's first permanent certification from Brazil's Ministry of Agriculture for grain handling, and record monthly throughput.
