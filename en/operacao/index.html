Where your operation loses money.

At a terminal, warehouse, mine or factory, the big gain rarely comes from new technology. It comes from what nobody has the patience to look at. The method is the Toyota Production System I implemented with TSSC, Toyota's own consulting arm, adapted to the reality of the Brazilian shop floor.

Method

Toyota Production System with Toyota itself

I led the TPS implementation with direct consulting from TSSC, the Toyota centre that brings the method to companies outside Japan. The terminal became VLI's TPS school.

In Brazil, TPS often arrives only halfway: tools and audits. The missing half is treating operators as people who think. That is the half I bring.

A test you can run tomorrow

Ask the operators on one shift what their biggest difficulty in the operation is. Then ask them to propose solutions.

If you are not surprised, either there is a problem with morale and culture, or the operation is already very well run.

Frequently asked questions

What kind of operation does the diagnostic work in?

Terminal, warehouse, yard, mine, plant or factory. The method is to look at the floor, measure times and movements at each step and turn every loss into money.

How long does it take?

Five working days in the standard format: context, field work, draft, validation with the team and presentation.

Do I need to stop the operation?

No. The work happens with the operation running, alongside operators, supervisors and indicators.

Do you implement Lean and TPS?

Yes. I implemented the Toyota Production System with TSSC and set up VLI's TPS school. The diagnostic is the entry point: it shows where the method pays off most in your operation. Implementation comes afterwards, as a follow-up engagement with its own scope.

What happens next?

You decide: carry out the plan on your own, hire support for the implementation, or commission a fixed-scope project. Each is scoped separately.
